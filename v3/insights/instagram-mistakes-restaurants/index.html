The five Instagram mistakes holding restaurants back

The Wonder Agency7 October 20265 min read

For most restaurants, Instagram is the first place a new guest looks. Before they book, they scroll: the food, the room, how busy you seem, whether it feels like their kind of place. It’s your shop window, menu and reputation in one.

We look at a lot of restaurant accounts, in audits and when new clients join us. The same five mistakes come up more than any others. All of them are fixable.

1. Posting without a plan

The most common pattern is a burst of posts, then nothing for three weeks, then a rushed post when someone remembers. Gaps make a restaurant look quiet, and rushed posts rarely show the food at its best.

The fix: plan a month at a time around what you need to sell, such as new dishes, quiet nights, set menus and big dates, and post on a steady rhythm you can keep up.

2. Food that doesn’t look as good as it tastes

Dim light, cluttered tables and plates shot from awkward angles make great food look ordinary. On a small screen, people judge in a second.

The fix: shoot in good light, keep the frame simple, and invest in a proper shoot every so often to build a library of strong images and video. Phone content works well alongside it, especially behind the scenes.

3. No people, no atmosphere

A feed of nothing but dishes can feel like a menu rather than a place. Guests are choosing an evening, not just a plate.

The fix: show the room filling up, the bar at its busiest, the chef at the pass and the team who look after people. That is what makes someone picture themselves there.

4. Not using video

Many restaurant accounts still post mostly still photos. Short vertical video, such as the sizzle, the pour or the plating, is what Instagram puts in front of new people, and it shows food in a way a photo can’t.

The fix: make reels a regular part of the plan. They don’t need to be complicated. A few seconds of something delicious happening is often enough.

5. Making it hard to book, and ignoring messages

Someone sees a reel, wants to come, and then can’t find how to book, or sends a message that sits unanswered for days. That is a booking lost at the very last step.

The fix: put the booking link in your profile and make it obvious, tell people how to book in captions when it matters, and answer comments and messages quickly. Treat your inbox like your phone line.

Where to start

If you only fix one thing, start with a plan. Most of the other problems come from posting in a hurry. And if you’d like an outside view, our free audit looks at your Instagram alongside your ads, search and website, and tells you what we’d change first.
